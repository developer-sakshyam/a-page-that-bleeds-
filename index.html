Don't stop reading.

BOOK ONE

THE MORNING
I DIDN'T

THE PAGES THAT BLEED

OPEN AT YOUR OWN RISK.

PROLOGUE

Before You Continue

There is something you should know before you continue.

Someone dies in this book.

Not later.

Not near the end.

Someone has already died.

You just haven't reached the page where you discover who.

The police will eventually call it murder.

The detective will eventually call it a pattern.

PROLOGUE — CONTINUED

I will eventually call it my fault.

But you?

You will call it a story.

That is why you are dangerous.

Because when people hear a scream through a wall, they investigate.

When they see blood on the floor, they run.

But when you see it written on a page—

you turn it.

And every time you turn one, something happens.

I know.

I've been waiting for you to start reading.

CHAPTER ONE

4:30 A.M.

Every morning, I wake up at 4:30.

At least, that's what I used to believe.

The alarm rings.

I turn it off.

I stare at the ceiling.

Then I begin another day pretending I know where my life is going.

College.

Projects.

Code.

CHAPTER ONE — CONTINUED

A future planned years before I have even reached it.

Sometimes I think too much about the future.

Not because I'm excited about it.

Because I don't trust the present enough to stay here.

So I plan.

I imagine another country.

Another house.

Another version of myself.

Someone successful.

CHAPTER ONE — CONTINUED

Someone who finally has enough money.

Enough freedom.

Enough distance.

Someone who no longer needs to wake up at 4:30 in the morning.

That morning began like every other.

The alarm rang.

I opened my eyes.

And there was someone sitting in the corner of my room.

CHAPTER ONE — CONTINUED

I stared at him.

He stared back.

Neither of us moved.

He was about my age.

Maybe.

It was difficult to tell because his face was hidden in darkness.

"Who are you?"

He tilted his head.

"You can see me?"

I didn't answer.

He smiled.

"Oh."

His voice became quieter.

"Then you're real."

CHAPTER TWO

I didn't tell anyone about him.

That sounds stupid now.

If a stranger appears in your room before sunrise, you tell someone.

You call the police.

You scream.

You run.

But there was something strange about him.

Something familiar.

CHAPTER TWO — CONTINUED

Not his face.

Not his voice.

His confidence.

He behaved like he had known me for years.

"You're late," he said the next morning.

"For what?"

"College."

I looked at the clock.

4:30 A.M.

CHAPTER TWO — CONTINUED

"How do you know my schedule?"

He laughed.

"You tell me everything."

"I don't know you."

His smile slowly disappeared.

"You don't remember?"

"No."

He looked genuinely afraid.

That should have frightened me.

Instead, I felt sorry for him.

That was my first mistake.

CHAPTER THREE

His name was Aarav.

Or at least that's what he told me.

He appeared only when nobody else was around.

My room.

Empty corridors.

The computer lab after everyone had left.

Once, I saw him sitting outside the college gate.

I stopped walking.

Cars passed between us.

CHAPTER THREE — CONTINUED

When the road cleared—

he was gone.

I started thinking he was imaginary.

Stress.

Lack of sleep.

My brain inventing someone to talk to.

It made sense.

Until Aarav asked me a question.

"Are you real?"

CHAPTER THREE — CONTINUED

I laughed.

"What kind of question is that?"

He wasn't laughing.

"Answer me."

"Of course I'm real."

He looked down.

For a moment, I thought he was going to cry.

Then he whispered:

"That's what I thought too."

CHAPTER FOUR

The first student disappeared three days later.

His name was Prakash.

Nobody knew where he went.

His phone was found near the computer lab.

His bag was still inside his classroom.

His parents came to college.

His mother screamed at the principal.

His father didn't say anything.

He just kept looking toward the hallway.

CHAPTER FOUR — CONTINUED

Like he expected his son to walk around the corner.

But Aarav knew something.

I saw it in his face.

"You know where he is."

"No."

"You looked at his picture."

"So?"

"You recognized him."

Aarav was silent.

CHAPTER FOUR — CONTINUED

I stepped closer.

"Tell me what you know."

He finally looked at me.

"I know he's not coming back."

My stomach dropped.

"How?"

Aarav stepped backward.

"Because he was talking to you yesterday."

CHAPTER FOUR — CONTINUED

I froze.

Prakash had been talking to me yesterday.

For almost an hour.

We had argued about a project.

A website.

Something completely meaningless.

But then I remembered something.

Something wrong.

I had never seen Prakash leave.

CHAPTER FIVE

The police arrived.

Then the detective.

His name was Detective Aryan.

He looked younger than I expected.

Calm.

Too calm.

He asked simple questions.

Where were you?

When did you last see him?

Did anything seem unusual?

CHAPTER FIVE — CONTINUED

Everyone answered.

Then he came to me.

"When did you last speak to Prakash?"

"Yesterday."

"What did you talk about?"

"A project."

"What project?"

I opened my mouth.

Nothing came out.

CHAPTER FIVE — CONTINUED

I remembered arguing.

I remembered being angry.

I remembered his voice.

But I couldn't remember what the project was.

Detective Aryan watched me carefully.

"That's strange."

"What?"

"Most people forget unimportant conversations."

CHAPTER FIVE — CONTINUED

He leaned closer.

"But you remember everything else."

I felt cold.

"What are you saying?"

He smiled.

"I'm saying someone has been editing your memories."

CHAPTER SIX

That night, I opened my laptop.

A document was already open.

I didn't recognize it.

The title was:

DAY 17

I clicked it.

The first sentence read:

Every morning, Sakshyam wakes up at 4:30.

CHAPTER SIX — CONTINUED

I stopped.

The second sentence read:

He does not yet understand why.

I scrolled.

The document contained everything that had happened.

College.

Aarav.

Prakash.

The detective.

CHAPTER SIX — CONTINUED

The conversation.

My room.

Even thoughts I had never spoken aloud.

I scrolled faster.

Then I reached the bottom.

There was a sentence I hadn't read before.

Sakshyam will now close the laptop and check the window.

I stared at it.

I didn't move.

For several seconds.

CHAPTER SIX — CONTINUED

Then I slowly looked toward the window.

Someone was standing outside.

Watching me.

I slammed the laptop shut.

My room went dark.

Aarav whispered from somewhere beside me.

"Don't worry."

I couldn't see him.

"Where are you?"

Silence.

CHAPTER SIX — CONTINUED

Then his voice came again.

"I'm still here."

"Where?"

A pause.

"Inside the part of your life you forgot."

CHAPTER SEVEN

The second body was found two days later.

This time there was no disappearance.

No mystery.

The student was dead.

Everyone knew it.

The police closed the computer lab.

The detective questioned everyone.

And someone had written something on the wall.

CHAPTER SEVEN — CONTINUED

Not with paint.

With blood.

HE READ IT TOO.

Nobody understood what it meant.

Except Aarav.

I found him sitting in the empty classroom.

"What does it mean?"

He didn't look at me.

"Sakshyam."

"What?"

CHAPTER SEVEN — CONTINUED

"Do you remember when we first met?"

"Yes."

"You asked me who I was."

"And?"

"You never asked me how I got into your room."

I felt something crawl beneath my skin.

Aarav slowly turned toward me.

"You knew I was already there."

"No."

"Yes."

"I didn't."

CHAPTER SEVEN — CONTINUED

His eyes filled with fear.

"You just forgot."

CHAPTER EIGHT

THE LOCKED ROOM

There was a room in my house I had never entered.

My mother had always told me not to open it.

Not when I was a child.

Not when I became older.

Never.

She never explained why.

CHAPTER EIGHT — CONTINUED

She just said:

"Some things are locked because they're dangerous."

After the second murder, I began noticing the door.

Every day.

Every night.

At the end of the hallway.

Locked.

Silent.

Waiting.

CHAPTER EIGHT — CONTINUED

One night, I heard someone knocking from inside.

Three knocks.

Then silence.

Three knocks.

Then silence.

I stood outside the door for almost ten minutes.

Then I heard my mother's voice.

"Sakshyam."

I froze.

CHAPTER EIGHT — CONTINUED

She was downstairs.

I had seen her downstairs.

But the voice came again.

"Sakshyam."

I unlocked the door.

And opened it.

My mother was sitting inside.

She looked terrified.

I stepped backward.

"Mom?"

CHAPTER EIGHT — CONTINUED

She stared at me.

Then she whispered:

"Don't let him see you."

"Who?"

Her eyes moved toward the hallway behind me.

"Your father."

My heart stopped.

"My father?"

She began crying.

CHAPTER EIGHT — CONTINUED

"Close the door."

"Why?"

"Because he doesn't know I'm still alive."

CHAPTER NINE

I ran downstairs.

My mother was sitting exactly where she had been.

Watching television.

I stared at her.

She looked back.

"What?"

I couldn't speak.

Then I looked toward the hallway.

The locked room was still open.

Something moved inside.

CHAPTER NINE — CONTINUED

I went back.

The room was empty.

No mother.

No furniture.

No windows.

Only writing on the wall.

My handwriting.

I recognized it immediately.

Thousands of words covered the room.

CHAPTER NINE — CONTINUED

One sentence repeated again and again.

I FOUND THE KILLER.

I FOUND THE KILLER.

I FOUND THE KILLER.

I FOUND THE KILLER.

But beneath the final sentence was something different.

I FORGOT WHO IT WAS.

CHAPTER TEN

Detective Aryan found me sitting outside the house.

He didn't ask what happened.

That was strange.

He simply sat beside me.

"You opened the room."

I looked at him.

"How do you know?"

He sighed.

"Because I told you not to."

CHAPTER TEN — CONTINUED

"You've never been here before."

Aryan looked confused.

Then frightened.

"No."

He stood.

"You're remembering things out of order again."

"What do you mean again?"

He froze.

I stood too.

CHAPTER TEN — CONTINUED

"What did you mean by again?"

Aryan didn't answer.

I grabbed his arm.

"How many times has this happened?"

His face changed.

And for the first time since I had met him—

the detective looked terrified of me.

CHAPTER ELEVEN

THE JOURNAL

The police eventually found the killer's journal.

They didn't tell the public.

They didn't tell the teachers.

They didn't tell the parents.

But Detective Aryan showed me.

I don't know why.

Maybe he trusted me.

Maybe he was afraid.

CHAPTER ELEVEN — CONTINUED

The journal was old.

Its pages were damaged.

Names were written everywhere.

Dates.

Locations.

Descriptions of people.

Some of the names belonged to victims.

Some belonged to people who were still alive.

And then I saw mine.

CHAPTER ELEVEN — CONTINUED

SAKSHYAM

Written hundreds of times.

I turned the page.

Then another.

Then another.

Every murder had been described before it happened.

The detective watched my face.

"What?"

I held up the journal.

"This handwriting."

CHAPTER ELEVEN — CONTINUED

"What about it?"

I looked at him.

My hands started shaking.

"It's mine."

CHAPTER TWELVE

I didn't sleep that night.

I sat in front of my laptop.

Watching the screen.

Waiting.

At 4:29 A.M., the computer turned on.

By itself.

A new document opened.

DAY 23

CHAPTER TWELVE — CONTINUED

One sentence appeared.

Sakshyam finally discovers the killer's journal.

I stopped breathing.

Another appeared.

He recognizes the handwriting.

Another.

He begins to suspect himself.

"No."

The next sentence appeared.

CHAPTER TWELVE — CONTINUED

He is wrong.

I stared at the screen.

Then another sentence appeared.

You are also wrong.

I slowly looked around the room.

There was nobody there.

The sentence continued.

The person reading this thinks they know where this is going.

CHAPTER TWELVE — CONTINUED

They think Sakshyam is the killer.

They think Aarav is imaginary.

They think Detective Aryan knows the truth.

They are wrong about all three.

Then the screen went black.

CHAPTER THIRTEEN

Aarav disappeared.

For three days.

I almost felt relieved.

Then I found him somewhere I had never seen him before.

In my childhood bedroom.

I hadn't entered that room in years.

He was sitting on the floor.

Playing with something.

"What are you doing here?"

CHAPTER THIRTEEN — CONTINUED

He looked up.

"I used to live here."

"No."

"Yes."

"This is my house."

Aarav smiled.

"I know."

"Then why are you saying you lived here?"

He became quiet.

CHAPTER THIRTEEN — CONTINUED

"Sakshyam."

"What?"

"Do you remember when you were younger?"

"Of course."

"No."

His voice cracked.

"Do you remember before you started calling this your life?"

I stared at him.

"What does that mean?"

CHAPTER THIRTEEN — CONTINUED

Aarav stood.

For the first time, he looked taller than me.

"You're not my imaginary friend."

I stopped.

He smiled sadly.

"I think..."

He looked toward the window.

Then back at me.

"I think I am yours."

CHAPTER FOURTEEN

That was when I began questioning everything.

Maybe Aarav was imaginary.

Maybe the detective was manipulating me.

Maybe I had written the journal.

Maybe I had committed the murders and forgotten.

But there was another possibility.

A worse one.

What if I wasn't the one imagining Aarav?

CHAPTER FOURTEEN — CONTINUED

What if Aarav had imagined me?

What if this entire life—

college,

my friends,

my plans,

my future,

Australia,

the house,

everything I was desperately trying to become—

existed inside someone else's imagination?

CHAPTER FOURTEEN — CONTINUED

I found Aarav later that night.

"Answer me honestly."

He nodded.

"Am I real?"

He didn't answer.

"Aarav."

His eyes filled with tears.

"Are you?"

CHAPTER FOURTEEN — CONTINUED

I wanted to scream at him.

But I couldn't.

Because suddenly I realized something.

I had never seen Aarav asleep.

I had never seen him eat.

I had never seen anyone introduce him.

And nobody—

not a single person—

had ever spoken his name before I did.

CHAPTER FIFTEEN

THE PAGE

Detective Aryan brought me something.

A page.

Just one.

It had been found at the newest crime scene.

There was blood around its edges.

But the writing was clear.

The first sentence read:

Now ask yourself who the real killer is.

CHAPTER FIFTEEN — CONTINUED

I looked at Aryan.

"What is this?"

He said nothing.

I continued reading.

If you hadn't turned the pages, no one would be dead.

My stomach dropped.

The detective looked at me.

"Do you understand it?"

"No."

"Good."

CHAPTER FIFTEEN — CONTINUED

I looked at him.

"What?"

"If you understood it already, we'd both be dead."

I stared at the page again.

There was more writing underneath.

But it wasn't addressed to me.

It was addressed to you.

You wanted another chapter.

You wanted another body.

You wanted to know what happened next.

So you continued.

CHAPTER FIFTEEN — CONTINUED

I closed the page.

My hands were shaking.

Aryan grabbed it.

"Don't."

I looked at him.

"What?"

"Don't stop."

"What are you talking about?"

His voice became desperate.

"Keep reading."

"This isn't a book."

His face went pale.

Then he whispered:

"How do you know?"

CHAPTER SIXTEEN

The next morning, Detective Aryan was missing.

His office was empty.

His car was abandoned.

The police said he had never worked with them.

That was impossible.

I had seen him.

Spoken to him.

He had investigated the murders.

I showed them his photograph.

Nobody recognized him.

CHAPTER SIXTEEN — CONTINUED

I opened the killer's journal.

Every page that mentioned Aryan was gone.

I returned home.

Aarav was waiting.

"You did this."

"No."

"You said he wasn't real."

"I didn't say that."

"You knew something would happen."

CHAPTER SIXTEEN — CONTINUED

Aarav looked exhausted.

"Sakshyam."

"What?"

"You're still asking the wrong question."

"Then tell me the right one."

He stared directly at me.

"Who is remembering who?"

CHAPTER SEVENTEEN

I returned to the locked room.

The door was already open.

Inside, the walls had changed.

The writing was gone.

There was only one sentence now.

DON'T FINISH THE BOOK.

I laughed.

I don't know why.

Maybe fear becomes funny when it gets too large.

CHAPTER SEVENTEEN — CONTINUED

Then another sentence appeared beneath it.

HE IS WAITING FOR THE LAST PAGE.

I stepped closer.

"Who?"

Nothing.

Then:

THE ONE WHO KILLED THE FIRST PERSON.

CHAPTER SEVENTEEN — CONTINUED

I stopped.

My mind returned to the first murder.

The body behind the computer lab.

The victim.

Prakash.

The beginning of everything.

But then I remembered something.

Something so obvious I almost missed it.

CHAPTER SEVENTEEN — CONTINUED

The first person hadn't been murdered.

Prakash had disappeared before anyone died.

Which meant—

the first murder happened before the story began.

Someone was already dead before you opened the first page.

Someone had died before I woke up.

Before Aarav.

Before the detective.

Before the journal.

Before everything.

CHAPTER SEVENTEEN — CONTINUED

And suddenly I understood why 4:30 A.M. mattered.

Not because that was when I woke up.

Because that was when someone else did.

CHAPTER EIGHTEEN

4:30

The alarm rang.

I opened my eyes.

Aarav was sitting in the corner.

But this time—

he wasn't looking at me.

He was looking toward the reader.

Toward you.

"Sakshyam?"

"What?"

CHAPTER EIGHTEEN — CONTINUED

"I think he's here."

"Who?"

Aarav pointed beyond the room.

Beyond the page.

I couldn't see anything.

But you can.

Can't you?

Aarav smiled.

Then whispered:

"I found him."

CHAPTER EIGHTEEN — CONTINUED

The lights went out.

My computer turned on.

One sentence appeared.

BOOK ONE ENDS HERE.

Then another.

THE KILLER HAS NOT BEEN REVEALED.

Then another.

BUT HE HAS BEEN INTRODUCED.

THE FINAL PAGES

I stared at the screen.

My heart stopped.

Because I realized something impossible.

The killer wasn't hiding.

He had been here the entire time.

Talking.

Watching.

Helping.

Maybe lying.

Maybe telling the truth.

Maybe appearing only when necessary.

THE FINAL PAGES — CONTINUED

But he had been here.

Somewhere.

On these pages.

You read his name.

You trusted him.

And when you begin Book Two—

you will probably read it again.

Because that is what you always do.

You keep turning pages.

Even after someone tells you that turning them is dangerous.

THE FINAL PAGES — CONTINUED

So go ahead.

Close this book.

The lights are going out.

Aarav is gone.

Detective Aryan is missing.

The locked room is open.

The journal is waiting.

And somewhere inside these pages—

the murderer is still alive.

THE FINAL PAGES — CONTINUED

I know.

Because he just whispered something to me.

He said:

"See you in Book Two."

And then he asked me something.

Something I still cannot answer.

Something you should ask yourself before you close this book.

If you were the one reading my life...

how did you know where I would end up?
